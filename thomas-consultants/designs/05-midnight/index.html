Consents and certificates on a typical development

Most land projects pass through the same approvals. We prepare the applications and the reports behind them. The decision always sits with the authority in the third column.

ApprovalWhat it coversIssued byWhenOur part
RCResource consentLand use or subdivision that the district plan does not allow as of right, under the Resource Management Act 1991The district or city council, and the regional council for some activitiesBefore detailed design and any worksRMA planning, plus engineering, landscape, ecology and contamination reports
EPAEngineering plan approvalThe design of roads, stormwater, wastewater and water supply that will become public assetsThe council, with the network operators. In Auckland: Auckland Council, Watercare and Auckland TransportAfter resource consent, before constructionEngineering design, drawings and applications
BCBuilding consentBuilding work, checked against the Building Code under the Building Act 2004A building consent authority, usually the councilBefore building work startsSite, drainage and civil engineering input, and compliance during construction
s223Survey plan approvalConfirms the survey plan matches the subdivision consentThe councilOnce the survey plan is prepared, usually late in the worksCadastral survey and the s223 plan
s224cCompletion certificateConfirms the subdivision consent conditions have been metThe councilAfter the works are finished and inspectedConstruction monitoring, as-built surveys and the completion documents
RTNew titlesA record of title for each new lotLand Information New ZealandAfter s223 and s224c are depositedSurvey dataset lodged with LINZ

Names and order vary between councils. This is the usual sequence for a subdivision in Auckland.

Health and safety on live sites

Our surveyors, engineers and ecologists spend much of the week on live construction sites, in streams and beside busy roads. Our health and safety system is certified to ISO 45001, and we are members of SiteSafe and SiteWise.

Construction monitoring is part of the same discipline. It manages risk while the works are built, so problems are found early instead of fixed after the fact.
